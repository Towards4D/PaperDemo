Abstract

High-fidelity street scene reconstruction is pivotal for end-to-end autonomous driving simulation, where novel-view synthesis and time-varying information modeling are the two key elements of 4D reconstruction tasks. Contrary to common belief, existing methods actually suffer from severe hidden rendering degradation and fail to achieve them simultaneously. We first establish an information-geometric diagnostic framework, revealing that this limitation stems from a credit assignment dilemma between spatial and temporal parameters. Specifically, the deterministic coupling between observing viewpoint and time in single-source data collecting creates a low-rank structure that induces massive null-space ambiguity between view-dependent and time-varying components. Temporal information overshadows spatial cues, causing the estimation variance of spatial parameters to diverge. To address this issue, we propose a hierarchical training method designed to restore spatial identifiability. It prioritizes the integrity of spatial representations by securing them in an initial stage, then restricts temporal updates to the spatial null space, enabling proactive credit assignment. A temporal regularization strategy is proposed to further refine the temporal solution space by imposing a smoothness constraint based on the physical prior of consistent appearance evolution. Experiments demonstrate that our method eliminates the hidden rendering degradation, maintaining stable NVS capabilities while modeling time-varying information.

Method

We first provide an information-geometric diagnostic framework to analyze the degree of ill-posedness of Gaussian Splatting under various scenarios. We then introduce a hierarchical training method to mitigate the ill-posedness of the problem by enabling proactive credit assignment. Finally, we present a temporal regularization strategy to further refine the temporal solution space, grounded in the physical prior of smooth temporal evolution.

Furthermore, a temporal regularization strategy grounds the solution in the physical prior that object appearance evolves smoothly and consistently over time. It enables transient information, observed only within a limited time window owing to visual occlusion, to be reasonably extrapolated over the entire time domain, constraining the solution space within a physically consistent domain.

Problems of Existing Methods

When performing novel-view observations, the synthesized images frequently suffer from rendering degradation, which can be attributed to two distinct causes. Type I degradation involves unobserved regions that were never captured during data collection. Type II degradation is a hidden degradation, overlooked by all existing methods: it is rooted in the intrinsic algorithmic limitations of reconstruction frameworks, where the introduction of time-varying modeling inevitably triggers rendering degradation. All current pipelines that incorporate time-varying dynamics fail to properly extrapolate transient observed information, leading to a degradation of even previously captured information when synthesized from novel viewpoints at novel timestamps.

Two existing paradigms

Reconstruction-based methods fall into two categories according to whether they model time-varying information. Paradigm I (e.g., StreetGaussians, S³Gaussian) introduces time-varying modeling yet assigns credit randomly, overfitting the open-loop trajectory and incurring type II degradation. Paradigm II (e.g., DrivingGaussian) models vehicles as time-invariant ideal rigid bodies, omitting temporal information.

Ablation Study

We ablate the core components of our framework: the hierarchical training method, the Temporal Variation (TV) penalty, and the choice of temporal basis. Applying either the hierarchical training method or the TV regularization alone already alleviates type II degradation to a certain extent — both are designed to better extrapolate the closed-loop surface c = F(d, t) from the open-loop curve c = F(γ(t), t), acting as alternative regularization schemes on the same surface from different perspectives — and combining them yields more stable performance.

Discussion: Fourier series vs. B-spline basis

In practice, storing full-order Fourier coefficients for every Gaussian is infeasible, and truncating the Fourier basis to low-frequency terms causes visible low-frequency flickering in the rendered frames. B-splines, whose local temporal support confines the influence of transient observations to specific time windows, eliminate this flickering, at a slight cost in reconstruction fidelity. We present this as a discussion of the engineering trade-off.
